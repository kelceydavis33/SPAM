SPAM adds nine medium bands and one wide band to the CEERS Early-Release Science field, bracketing the Balmer break and tracing [O III] emission out to the earliest known reionized bubbles.

Abstract

SPAM (Star-formation from Photometry through the Addition of Medium-bands) is a NIRCam imaging program adding ten filters — F070W, F140M, F162M, F182M, F210M, F300M, F335M, F360M, F430M and F480M — to the CEERS Early-Release Science legacy dataset (ERS #1345).

The program is built around three goals:

  1. Measure the extent and occupation of two overdensities that may power the earliest-known reionized bubbles at z = 7.7 and 8.7, by tracing strong [O III] emission and improving photometric-redshift precision by 5–10× over CEERS alone.
  2. Break photometric redshift degeneracies and reliably map galaxy evolution at z > 2.
  3. Constrain star-formation histories of massive galaxies at z > 4 using five short-wavelength medium bands bracketing the Balmer break.

All 62.9 hours are non-proprietary and add directly to the public CEERS legacy data.
